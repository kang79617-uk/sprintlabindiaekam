Weekly training plan

Pick a day. Warm up for 20 minutes before every session: jog, dynamic mobility, drills (A-skips, B-skips, high knees), and 2–3 build-up strides.

Rules that make the plan work

Sprinting is a skill trained fresh. These are the habits behind the sessions.

Full recovery between reps

Rest 1 minute per 10 metres on speed work. Rushing rest turns speed training into conditioning.

Quality over volume

Stop a speed session when times drop by more than 3–5%. Extra reps on tired legs build bad mechanics.

Sleep 8–9 hours

Muscle repair and nervous system recovery happen at night. It is the cheapest performance gain available.

Progress gradually

Add load or distance by about 5–10% a week, and take an easier week every 4th week.

Before bedWarm milk or casein-rich yoghurt to support overnight repair.

Eat more of

  • Rice, potatoes, oats, roti and fruit for training fuel
  • Eggs, dairy, fish, chicken, lentils and tofu for protein
  • Nuts, seeds, olive oil and oily fish for healthy fats
  • Colourful vegetables and citrus for micronutrients

Limit

  • Deep-fried food and sweets close to training
  • Large fibre-heavy meals within 2 hours of speed sessions
  • Energy drinks and heavy caffeine late in the day
  • Alcohol, which slows recovery and disrupts sleep

Hydration: drink steadily through the day and check that urine is pale yellow. On hot days add a pinch of salt or an electrolyte drink around training.

This plan is general information for healthy adults. Check with a coach, doctor or sports dietitian before starting, especially if you are returning from injury, under 18, or have a medical condition. Stop any session that causes sharp pain.
